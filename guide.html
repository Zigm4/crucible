User Guide

Crucible is a single open-source page that talks directly to live WAX smart contracts. The NeftyBlocks, WaxDAO and Blenderizer websites went down, but the contracts behind them never did - Crucible is the missing UI. Zero backend, no tracking, no fee to anyone but the contracts' own (unchanged) fees. Your wallet signs every transaction; the page never sees a key.

Before you start

  1. Connect a wallet - Anchor or WAX Cloud Wallet. Connecting is read-only until you sign.
  2. Stake a little CPU/NET. Nefty used to sponsor your CPU; that sponsor no longer signs, so keep roughly 5-10 WAX staked to CPU (and a little to NET) from your wallet's Resources tab.
  3. Pick a theme (top-right): Neon, Clair, or Sombre. Your choice is remembered.
“greymassfuel@cosign … does not have signatures for it”? That's your wallet trying to borrow CPU through Greymass Fuel and not getting cosigned - stake a few WAX to CPU, or sign with WAX Cloud Wallet, and it goes away. It is not a Crucible bug.

Blend · blend.nefty

Burn NFTs (and optionally pay tokens) to mint a result. Type a collection, click Discover blends, pick one, choose the NFTs for each ingredient slot, and sign.

  • Deterministic blends mint in a single signature.
  • Random blends (more than one possible outcome) take two: one to commit, then - after the on-chain oracle resolves - one to claim. Crucible waits in between and shows you the resolved result before step 2.
  • Whitelisted blends are detected automatically; the picker marks whether your wallet is allowed before you try.

Claim · neftyblocksd

Drops - pay (or not) to mint. Crucible signs public, whitelist, and NFT-proof drops:

  • Public - open to anyone.
  • Whitelist - your account must be on the drop's allow-list.
  • NFT proof - you must hold specific NFTs; the page auto-selects matching ones and tells you exactly what's required even if you don't have them.

Free drops skip the payment step. Key-gated (claimdropkey) drops need a per-user secret only the creator can issue, so no third-party tool can sign them.

Unpack · atomicpacksx + neftyblocksp

Opens packs from both pack contracts - AtomicHub (atomicpacksx) and NeftyBlocks (neftyblocksp) - merged into one list. It's a two-signature, oracle-randomised flow:

  1. Send the pack to the contract (memo unbox).
  2. Wait 5-30s while the ORNG oracle resolves the contents.
  3. Sign once more to mint the revealed cards.

Crucible scans both contracts globally and only lists collections where your wallet holds an openable pack, then uses the right open/claim flow for that pack automatically.

Upgrade · up.nefty

Mutate NFTs you already own in place (e.g. recolour, level up) by paying the upgrade's cost. Token-cost upgrades are signable today; random-result and whitelist/ownership-gated upgrades are detected and clearly tagged as not-yet-executable.

WaxDAO Blend · waxdaomarket

WaxDAO is a parallel ecosystem with its own blend contract. Switch to the WAXDAO platform pill at the top; recipes are listed per collection and built with WaxDAO's own action shape (one transfer per ingredient slot).

Blenderizer · blenderizerx

A third ecosystem, and despite the name not a Nefty contract: the Blenderizer is 3DkRender's, and it predates blend.nefty. Switch to the BLENDERIZER platform pill at the top and discover recipes per collection.

Recipes are the simplest of the three: burn a fixed list of templates, mint one target. No odds, no pool, no whitelist, no token cost, and one signature - a single atomicassets::transfer carrying every NFT at once. The contract mints and burns in the same transaction, so there is no waiting and no claim step.

A recipe's id is the template it mints, so #/blenderizer/blend/336429 and “template 336429” are the same thing.

Two things can stop a recipe paying out, and Crucible checks both before you deposit: a capped target that is fully minted (tagged sold out), and a collection with no RAM balance on the contract - blenderizerx mints from RAM the collection author pre-paid, so an unfunded collection fails every blend until they top it up.

NFT artwork

Blends, drops, packs, upgrades, WaxDAO blends, Blenderizer recipes and the catalogue all show the artwork of what you are about to get. It sits beside the numbers, never on top of them: the supply, the cost and the status stay the first thing you read.

Artwork is stored on-chain only as an IPFS hash, so it is fetched from a public IPFS gateway - the one request Crucible makes that is about you rather than about the chain. If every gateway is unreachable, the image is dropped and the card renders exactly as it would without it. Nothing waits on an image.

Collection catalogue · #/catalog

The tabs above split Crucible by contract, which is what you want when you are about to sign something. When you are just browsing, that is the wrong cut: you want boots, and you do not care whether they come from a blend, a Blenderizer recipe, a drop or a pack.

The catalogue flips it. Pick a collection and it scans all six contracts at once, then groups everything by category - the schema of the item produced (up.armour, up.resources…) - with a coloured badge showing which contract each row lives on. One click switches the grouping back to per-contract.

With a wallet connected, every entry your NFTs can actually satisfy right now is marked ✓ ready and sorted to the top, and the ones you cannot do say what you are missing (“missing 3× template 218122”). Tick only what I can do and the page becomes a to-do list.

It is read-only: clicking a row opens it in the normal tab, where the usual flow does the signing. Reach it from the link under the platform pills, from the footer, or directly at #/catalog/underpunks55 - which is shareable.

Collection authors

If your connected wallet is an authorized account of a collection, Crucible reveals inline management controls (each behind a safety toggle). The contracts always allowed this - only the website was missing.

Creating blends and upgrades

Creating a new blend or upgrade is not on this page. It runs on a separate guided screen that reads your collection first, so templates are picked from a list with their artwork instead of typed from memory, the odds are drawn as a bar, and an upgrade's attribute types are read from the schema rather than declared by hand. Ask the maintainer for the address if you author a collection.

Why it moved. Creation is permanent and costs RAM, and having two ways to do it meant two sets of mistakes to guard against. The guided screen simulates the exact transaction against the live contract before anything is signed, and refuses recipes the chain would accept but no player could ever use.

Editing a blend afterwards

Everything except the recipe itself can be changed after creation, from the Manage panel: name and image, category, time window, max uses, per-account limit, whitelist, hidden flag - and deleting it outright.

The ingredients can now be edited too, from the Manage panel. The box is pre-filled with the recipe as it stands on chain, because the action replaces the whole list - anything you delete from the box stops being required.

The outcomes cannot be changed by an author at all. That is a contract limit, not a missing button: setrolls takes no authorized_account and has only ever been called by NeftyBlocks' own accounts, so your signature would be rejected. To change what a blend produces, delete it and create a new one.

Manage blends & whitelists (BLEND tab)

Rename, hide, set the time window, change limits, delete, and manage reusable named whitelists (secure.nefty): create one, fill it with wallets, and attach it to one or several blends.

Create & manage drops (CLAIM tab)

  • Create a drop from existing templates with all the standard options. Risky choices (which templates get minted, unlimited/free supply, the payout account) are boxed in red with the reason.
  • Manage a drop - load any drop you manage (pick it from “drops I can manage”, or by id) to edit its whitelist, toggle gating, hide, or delete it.
A drop's whitelist is per-drop: creating a drop with “whitelist required” starts it empty (nobody can claim) - add the wallets afterwards in Manage a drop. The drop you just made loads there automatically.

Shareable links

Every entity has a hash URL you can paste to anyone (no wallet needed to read a recipe):

  • #/nefty/blend/43444 - a blend
  • #/nefty/claim/237418 - a drop
  • #/nefty/upgrade/447 - an upgrade
  • #/waxdao/blend/1921 - a WaxDAO blend
  • #/blenderizer/blend/336429 - a Blenderizer recipe (id = target template)

Privacy & safety

  • No backend, no database, no tracking. One page that reads the chain and builds transactions.
  • Your keys never leave your wallet. Anchor and WAX Cloud Wallet sign locally.
  • 0% cut. The blend/drop/upgrade fees that always existed still go where they always went.
  • Open source. Read every line, fork it, host it yourself - that's the whole point.
